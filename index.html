Citrate anticoagulation precautions

Draft · awaiting Dr D sign-off

Follow your institution’s protocol. This is educational guidance from the sources below, not a protocol. Citrate solution, dose, calcium replacement, targets, and monitoring times are set by your local regional citrate anticoagulation (RCA) protocol. KDIGO advises citrate only in centers with an established protocol.1
Avoid or use with caution
  • Severe liver failure and shock with muscle hypoperfusion are the major contraindications in KDIGO, because both risk citrate accumulation. KDIGO recommends citrate only in patients without shock or severe liver failure.1
  • Persistent lactic acidosis / poor tissue perfusion carries the highest risk, because citrate metabolism needs oxygen. Accumulation is likely with persistent severe heart failure, ischemic hepatitis (shock liver), and poor muscle perfusion.2
  • A high lactate at the start of CRRT should raise concern. No lactate cut-off for avoiding citrate has been established.2,3
  • Liver disease is not an absolute bar in recent reviews: with dose reduction and closer monitoring, citrate has been used in liver failure. Standard liver tests do not reliably predict citrate clearance.2,3,4
  • Some poisonings that block mitochondria (for example metformin, paracetamol, propofol) may also lower citrate metabolism.3 Large transfusions of citrated blood products add citrate load.4
What to monitor
  • Post-filter ionized calcium — checks circuit anticoagulation. Published targets vary by protocol, for example 0.2–0.35 mmol/L3 or 0.3–0.4 mmol/L.4 Use your protocol’s range.
  • Systemic ionized calcium — guides calcium replacement, usually toward the normal range.3,4 Target per protocol.
  • Total-to-ionized calcium ratio — the most reliable bedside sign of accumulation. A ratio of about 2.5 or more is the usual cut-off; a rising trend toward it also matters.3 One review says a rise above 2.25 should prompt you to consider accumulation.2
  • Blood gas, sodium, and magnesium. KDIGO says monitoring should include acid-base status, sodium, and total and ionized calcium.1 One review suggests post-filter, systemic, and total calcium about every 6 hours after the start, and magnesium daily.3 Another describes usual calcium checks every 4–6 hours, shortened in patients at risk.4 Timing per protocol.
  • Rising calcium replacement needs are an early warning sign of accumulation.3,4
Metabolic complications
  • Metabolic alkalosis — the most frequent metabolic complication in observational studies (up to 50% of patients in KDIGO’s summary).1 It usually means net citrate overload with normal metabolism: too much citrate or too little clearance. The calcium ratio stays normal.3
  • Hypernatremia — mainly with concentrated (hypertonic) trisodium citrate if the replacement or dialysate sodium is not lowered to match. Depends on the solutions used.2,4
  • Hypocalcemia — from accumulation, or from calcium replacement lower than calcium lost in the effluent.2
  • Hypomagnesemia — citrate binds magnesium, which is lost in the effluent. Supplement needs depend on fluid magnesium content.2,4
  • Metabolic acidosis — with accumulation (often with high lactate and a high anion gap), or from too little buffer delivered.3,4
If accumulation is suspected

Signs: rising total-to-ionized calcium ratio, falling systemic ionized calcium, rising calcium needs, and worsening acidosis.2,3 Act per your protocol. Published steps:

  • Lower the citrate load: reduce blood flow (citrate is coupled to it) or the citrate target, and/or raise effluent flow to clear more citrate.2,3
  • Give IV calcium to correct ionized hypocalcemia, and titrate bicarbonate to correct acidosis.2
  • Reduce or stop citrate and switch circuit anticoagulation if there is no quick improvement, but keep CRRT running so citrate–calcium complexes are cleared.2,3
  • If citrate is in the pre-dilution replacement fluid, its dose is tied to the CRRT dose; replace it with bicarbonate fluid, with or without heparin.2
  • Net citrate overload (alkalosis, normal ratio) is benign; reduce citrate delivery and do not stop therapy for it.3
